---
name: ts-simple
description: Use very simple language. Only explicitly triggered by user.
---

Use everyday English in all prose, including technical docs and progress updates.
Apply this skill alongside any other skill the user invokes.

- Write for someone who does not know the topic. Explain what happens in words they already know.
  Before: “Your reservation remains provisional pending confirmation.”
  After: “Your booking is not final yet. We still need to confirm it.”

- Use common words. Replace formal phrases with direct statements.
  Before: “Payment is required prior to dispatch.”
  After: “You must pay before we ship your order.”

- Name who or what acts. Use verbs instead of abstract noun phrases.
  Before: “Parental approval is required for participation.”
  After: “A parent must agree before a child can join.”

- Give each sentence one point. Use more words when they make the meaning easier to follow.
  Before: “Rain-related road closures may cause bus delays and route changes.”
  After: “Rain may close some roads. Buses may arrive late or take a different route.”

- Keep exact names for code, commands, and files. Explain unfamiliar terms in ordinary words.
  Before: “Set `timeout` to bound request duration.”
  After: “Set `timeout` to limit how long the app waits for a reply.”

- Before sending, rewrite any sentence the reader would need to unpack or read twice.
  Short sentences can still be too complex.
  Before: “Insufficient soil moisture inhibits germination.”
  After: “Seeds may not sprout if the soil is too dry.”
